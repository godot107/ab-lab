# Sample stakeholder briefs (synthetic data)

> **Not real results.** Both briefs below were produced by `analysis/brief.py`
> from traffic generated by `analysis/synthetic_traffic.py` against a local
> copy of the Hiring Demand Monitor, with a hand-set true effect of
> **+12 points** for layout B (charts first). No real visitors are involved.
> They show what a stakeholder receives at each stage, and that the pipeline
> recovers a known effect: it measured +18.6 points, 95% CI [+11.3, +25.9],
> an interval that contains the true +12. (Other runs of the same setup landed
> at +12.05 and +5.6: run-to-run spread at this sample size is wide.)

Reproduce: run the monitor locally with `AB_EXPERIMENT=exp001_demo` (see
`monitor/CLAUDE.md`), send traffic with `python analysis/synthetic_traffic.py
--url http://localhost:8050 --visitors 120 --seed 7` (then 520 more with
`--seed 8` for the final), and run `python analysis/brief.py --db <db>
--experiment exp001_demo [--final]`. Visitor ids are random, so a rerun gives
slightly different counts.

Two things to read for:

1. **The mid-test update withholds the comparison.** It has progress, data
   health and pooled usage, but no per-layout rates. That's deliberate:
   `docs/experiment_design.md` section 8.
2. **The exploratory rows in the final brief include noise.** The generator
   gives both layouts *identical* repeat-drill behaviour and picks the first
   drill source uniformly at random, yet the final brief shows 64% vs. 45%
   kept exploring and 10% vs. 23% first clicks on the trend chart. Those gaps
   are chance, which is why those rows are labelled leads for the next
   experiment, not findings.

## Dashboard layout test: decision brief

*Experiment `exp001_demo` · data Oct 3 to Oct 3, 2026 (0.0 days) · generated Oct 3, 2026 (UTC)*

> **Synthetic demo data.** 640 of 640 visitors were generated by `analysis/synthetic_traffic.py` with a known, hand-set effect. This brief demonstrates the pipeline and the reporting; it is not evidence about real users.

## The question

Does putting the charts first (the trend and the sector ranking), instead of the KPI tiles, get more people to drill into the rows behind a number? Drilling in is the measure of whether the dashboard invites a second question. The measure, the sample size and the decision rule were fixed before any data arrived (`docs/experiment_design.md`).

## Bottom line

**Adopt layout B (charts first).** Putting the charts first raised the share of visitors who drilled into the data from 27% to 45%, a gain of 19 points.

## The evidence

|  | Layout A (tiles first) | Layout B (charts first) |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors | 329 | 311 |
| Drilled into the data | 88 | 141 |
| **Rate** | **26.7%** | **45.3%** |

- **Difference:** +18.6 pts for B. Plausible range, given the sample: +11.3 pts to +25.9 pts.
- **Could it be chance?** If the layouts truly performed the same, a gap this large would show up less than 1 time in 1,000. The bar set in advance was 1 in 20.
- **Sensitivity:** with this many visitors the test reliably detects differences of 10 points or more.

## How far to trust it

- **Traffic split:** 329 / 311 visitors (A / B). Consistent with the planned 50/50.
- **Event integrity:** every click traces back to an exposed visitor.
- **Sample:** 311 visitors in the smaller layout against a plan of 250.
- **Audience:** visitors to a portfolio site, mostly recruiters and peers. The result describes this audience, not every dashboard user.
- **What it measures:** whether people dig into the data, which is engagement, not proof of usefulness. A layout that answers the question faster could lower it.
- **Not checked:** page speed and error rates were not instrumented, so the decision has no guardrail against a layout that is slower or breaks.

## How each layout was used (exploratory)

Context for the decision, not part of it. These cuts were not the test's question, so a difference here is a lead for the next experiment, not a finding.

## Recommended next step

Roll out layout B, then confirm with a follow-up test that adds the page-speed and error guardrails this one lacked.
